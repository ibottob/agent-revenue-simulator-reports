The Engine

Why stop at registered customers? We simulate the entire addressable market.

Every registered customer becomes a behavioral twin — but ZENSUS census data lets us model everyone in the surrounding population who could become one. The LLM reads the data, infers parameters, and builds the agent — for 100% of the database plus the full market.

What we know about one customer
  • Typical usage times and trip patterns
  • Price elasticity from past bookings
  • Customer service interactions and sentiment
  • Age, home geoposition, household profile
  • Socio-economic environment and local competition
What ZENSUS adds for non-customers
  • Household income and composition by area
  • Commuting patterns and modal split
  • Car ownership rates per district
  • Population density, age distribution
  • Anybody in the market — modeled as potential agent
What a human would do
Read the profile, infer parameters, tune the twin, repeat — for all customers.
3 hours per customer ~210,000 hours total for 70K
What the LLM does
LLM generates each twin once, then structured inference takes over. The simulation itself runs as pure code — no LLM during simulation.
Minutes per customer Full fleet generated in hours
~€3K compute cost per operator for full twin set (LLM generation only, simulation runs as code). Regenerated only when the customer base changes significantly.

The LLM is not guessing. It does what a data scientist would do — reading profiles, inferring parameters, building behavioral models — for every single person in the database, then layers ZENSUS data to model the rest of the market.
