Methodology

Random Search was chosen over gradient methods because the simulation is a black-box function: no derivatives, high noise (stochastic demand), and mixed continuous/discrete parameters. Each configuration is a point in 14-dimensional JointState space; we sample {{CONFIGS}} points uniformly at random and evaluate each with {{SEEDS}} independent random seeds to average out simulation noise.

Why not Simulated Annealing (v3)? SA converges faster but obscures the landscape — you get one path, not the shape. Random search gives every parameter combination equal visibility, which is essential for traceability and sensitivity analysis.

Simulation fidelity: Each run is a full 365-day agent-based simulation with 2,000 customer twins, 81 vehicles, 35 Aachen stations. Twin behavior is calibrated against 2024 historical booking data. No extrapolation — every number is a simulated outcome.

Assumptions & Limitations

  • Demand model calibrated on 2024 Aachen data; structural shifts (new competitors, regulations) are not modeled.
  • Fleet reallocation assumes zero relocation cost.
  • Marketing campaign effect is modeled as linear awareness boost; real-world saturation or diminishing returns not captured.
  • Cost structure held constant across all configurations.
