What was tested?

The optimizer explored 15 independent random scenarios, each running 40 simulated annealing steps. Every step evaluates a complete 365-day operation: 2,000 digital twins generate realistic booking demand against 81 vehicles across 35 stations. This produces roughly 5,000–8,000 individual demand events per step that the supply-matching engine resolves minute-by-minute.

Methodology (Non-Technical)

Think of the simulator as a “digital wind tunnel” for your car-sharing business. We built 2,000 virtual customer profiles calibrated against your historical Aachen booking data. Each has realistic preferences: how far in advance they book, how long they keep the car, which station they prefer, how sensitive they are to price.

The optimizer then systematically tweaks pricing and fleet allocation and re-runs the wind tunnel thousands of times. Unlike a spreadsheet model, this captures supply-and-demand dynamics: if a station is fully booked on a given day, a new customer either takes a similar car elsewhere (fallback) or walks away (lost revenue). This makes the forecast far more reliable than simple extrapolation.

Monte Carlo detail: Each optimization step is one full 365-day year simulation. With 15 seeds × 40 steps, we executed roughly 30 complete year-long simulations (15 seeds each ran 40 steps; plus one baseline). Each simulation processes 2,000 customer decision trajectories. In total, this represents approximately 1,200,000 demand events tested across the search space.

Limitations & Caveats

1. Assumes historical demand patterns hold. If a new competitor opens or macroeconomic conditions shift, the model should be re-calibrated.

2. Weekend factor tested in isolation. Combined effects with yield management or marketing campaigns were also evaluated but showed negative interaction — see lever table below.

3. Stochastic results vary by ±€10k. The profit figure €63,083 is the result of one verified run; re-running the same configuration with a different random stream could yield ±€10k due to demand variability.
